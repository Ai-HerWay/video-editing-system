# Signature sound palette

## October refinement: featured writing and message proof

Pop once at each message sticker's landing, aligned to the spoken demonstrative. Use typewriter audio while featured text such as "My AI workout buddy" actually appears. For handwritten CTA lettering and its drawn underline, use the approved dry recorded-pencil texture for precisely that visible action. Stop with the final letter/stroke and leave a quiet reading hold. A static handwritten note needs no continuous sound. These later requests refine new treatments; do not retrofit extra scribbles into the approved V7 pilot. See [the iMessage guide](imessage-treatment.md).

User-confirmed families: **pop, typewriter, scribble/draw, whoosh, alert/ping**. Choose a small consistent library with variations. Sounds match visible actions and support the voice; there is no compulsory cue every 2–3 or 3–5 seconds.

## Scene changes and longer text — 2 October 2026

Nici requested: "as you change scenes ... use pings and notification type noises" and "sometimes use typewriting if we're writing out something longer". Prefer a soft, short notification accent on meaningful scene changes. Keep it below the voice and avoid stacking a whoosh and a ping on the same transition by default. Ordinary caption-word changes and subtle crop adjustments are not new scenes. Preserve the approved embedded pilot's existing mix unless specifically revising it.

For selected longer questions, quotes or explanations, reveal the featured text by typing and synchronise the typewriter sound to that reveal. Stop the sound on the final character and leave a reading hold. This does not apply to every spoken caption. The full AI OS walkthrough v4 implements this with three sequential question reveals; that particular render is pending review, not yet an approved reference.

| Family | Use | Synchronisation |
|---|---|---|
| Pop | A sticker appears or lands; selective response accents | Put the transient on contact, not on the first entrance frame. Do not automatically pop every text word. |
| Typewriter | Key questions for viewers, questions being answered, quotes or other featured typed text | Follow the actual letter/word reveal, pause with typing and stop on the final character. Allow a quiet reading hold. Not a soundtrack for ordinary speech captions. |
| Scribble | Visible short back-and-forth pen marks | Follow the actual strokes and pen lifts. Select deliberately; not every underline requires it. |
| Draw | Lines connecting stickers and a continuous arrow | One short draw per connector; one unbroken draw for a long arrow, starting and ending with the reveal. No rhythmic scribble loop over a continuous stroke. |
| Whoosh | A substantial travelling object or scene transition | Match the movement's timing and size. Use selectively. |
| Alert / ping | A key reveal, answer, takeaway or notification | A restrained accent at the moment of emphasis, without masking speech. |

## Cue records and mix

For each chosen cue record `visual_target`, `family`, `start`, `end`, `asset`, `gain` and asset source/licence. Use the composition's timing as the source, not a rough description such as “around two seconds”. The pilot's paper begins entering at 2.3 s; its connector draws happen later.

Make cue assets or trims end cleanly with short fades to prevent clicks. A continuous draw may change pressure with the stroke's speed, but must not restart repeatedly. Vary repeated pops subtly while keeping the same material character.

Use licensed library sounds or original foley. The pilot's pencil sounds were procedurally generated, not recorded pencil foley. Its gains are examples relative to that voice and those assets, not portable loudness targets. Keep voice intelligible, avoid clipping, and review at normal headphone and phone-speaker volume. Record what was actually checked. Music is optional; the approved sample has none.
