# Layout modes

Pick the mode from what the frame actually shows you, not from the kind of media it is.

## The three modes

**`available-area`** — choose this when a stable pocket of negative space is big enough to hold the complete idea. Anchor a deliberate editorial stage inside that region and leave the footage and source UI untouched.

**`direct-overlay`** — choose this when footage fills the frame, or when the negative space shifts too much to trust. Work on a bounded surface, a local scrim, or strong type contrast, and stay clear of faces and load-bearing source content.

**`full-frame`** — choose this when there is no footage, or when a beat deliberately hands the whole frame to graphics. Use the warm canvas, one hero idea, and a single secondary object — a UI element or a structural path.

A mode is chosen per beat and never switched mid-beat. Across a composition, different beats may run different modes when the source and the story call for it.

## Inspecting before you commit

1. Capture or inspect at least three representative frames — early, middle, late.
2. Mark the face and mouth regions, the logos, the source UI, the source text, the captions, and anything moving.
3. Find the regions that stay clear across all of the sampled frames.
4. Reserve the caption lane before you place any other graphic.
5. Only reach for `available-area` when the full motion envelope fits, overshoot and cursor travel included.
6. Fall back to `direct-overlay` when the clear region is intermittent or too small for the whole idea.

## Honouring an existing design spec

- When the user explicitly invokes this style, treat it as the presentation authority.
- Keep approved identity assets, the user's exact copy, and any semantic brand information.
- Preserve an existing `frame.md` once as `frame.pre-quiet-editorial-ui.md` before replacing it — and never back up a backup.
- Record any retained brand exceptions under `Approved Entities` in the working `frame.md`.

## How each aspect behaves

- **Portrait** — favour a strong vertical stack or a bounded stage. Do not assume graphics on top and footage below.
- **Landscape** — favour an asymmetric editorial split when a stable subject holds one side; otherwise use a low, wide overlay or a full-frame treatment.
- **Square** — use a compact vertical hierarchy and fewer simultaneous UI objects.
- **Busy or bright footage** — put a warm-white surface or a localised contrast layer behind load-bearing text.
- **Clean footage** — allow unboxed ink text as long as contrast and motion stay readable.
