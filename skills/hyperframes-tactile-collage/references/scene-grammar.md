# Scene grammar

Choose the physical metaphor from the narrative function, not from a fixed storyboard.

| Narrative function | Preferred collage construction | Motion cause |
|---|---|---|
| Hook or surprising claim | Expanding cutout cluster plus one stamped verdict | Objects gather, then the verdict lands |
| Many alternatives | Mismatched tabs, cards, or labels around one anchor | Fan out from a shared origin |
| New concept | Taped note, open notebook spread, or lifted paper panel | The page opens or the note is attached |
| Person or evidence | Taped photo with a compact evidence badge | Route draws toward the evidence |
| Comparison | Two unequal paper stations with a central divider | Cards separate, then settle into roles |
| Process | Dotted path, numbered cards, or stacked checklist | Each step appears because the previous completes |
| Handoff | A file tag or object physically crosses between stations | The transferred object owns the transition |
| Transformation | Preserve one card's position while its content or state changes | The existing object becomes the result |
| Proof or result | Filed documents, checked rows, receipt, or approved stamp | Evidence stacks, checks, or stamps in |
| Return or reuse | Folder reopens and points back to earlier material | A route closes into a loop |
| Closing prompt | Two or three large paper questions, not a dashboard grid | Questions land in spoken order |

## Beat construction

1. Write the beat's single sentence.
2. Identify its concrete noun or action.
3. Choose one physical metaphor that makes that noun or action visible.
4. Design the strongest paused frame.
5. Add at most one hero move, one support move, and restrained background motion.
6. End with a readable state that can hand an object, direction, color, or shape into the next beat.

## Continuity

- Reuse paper size, edge weight, and shadow direction across the piece.
- Carry important objects across beats when the story refers to the same thing.
- Give repeated concepts stable colors and shapes.
- Let the photographic subject remain the human anchor; do not recenter every graphic around the speaker.
- Use full-frame collage moments deliberately to reset attention, not on every sentence.
